# Google Play Store & Online Connectivity Guide (Days 3–30)

Starting with **Day 3**, every app generated by the Daily Android App Factory is designed to be **online-capable (with offline caching)** and **Google Play Store ready & downloadable**.

---

## 1. Privacy by Default (Opt-In Only) + Online Resilience
Apps from Day 3 onward follow **zero unsolicited user data** rules:
- **Stateless by Default:** Local history saving (`SharedPreferences`) and online API/webhook calls are **OFF by default** until the user explicitly enables an **Opt-In** toggle (or taps an explicit action button), with a 1-tap **"Wipe All Local Data"** button and zero device IDs or analytics SDKs.
- **Network Permissions:** `android.permission.INTERNET` and `android.permission.ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`.
- **Zero-Bloat Networking:** Uses standard Android/JDK `javax.net.ssl.HttpsURLConnection` inside `kotlinx.coroutines.Dispatchers.IO` (with `10_000 ms` timeouts and `instanceFollowRedirects = false`) and `org.json.JSONObject` / `JSONArray`—keeping Gradle builds clean and fast in Google AI Studio without external HTTP/serialization dependency resolution failures.
- **Free, Keyless Public APIs & Webhooks:** Each day pairs its hardware/DSP core with an optional opt-in live online feature from `30_DAY_SLATE.md` (e.g., Ntfy.sh/Discord webhooks, NOAA SWPC space weather, Open-Meteo, Open-Elevation, OpenFoodFacts, RIPEstat/RDAP, Frankfurter FX, Free Dictionary API, OSM Nominatim).
- **Optional Local Cache:** Only when the user opts in to caching/saving, fetched payloads are stored locally in `MODE_PRIVATE` `SharedPreferences` with timestamps and clear `ONLINE` vs. `OFFLINE` badges.

---

## 2. Google AI Studio Download Workflow
Once Google AI Studio finishes compiling an app cleanly:
1. The automated factory clicks **"Download app"** in Google AI Studio (`aistudio.google.com/apps`) to export the compiled Android project/bundle to your local Downloads folder.
2. Each day also saves a ready-to-use `play_store_listing.md` inside `days/day_NN_<slug>/` containing:
   - App Title (≤ 30 chars)
   - Short Description (≤ 80 chars)
   - Full Description (≤ 4000 chars)
   - Play Store Category & Tags
   - Google Play Data Safety Questionnaire answers (matching exact permissions & API calls)
   - Signing & AAB Release Checklist

---

## 3. Packaging & Signing an `.aab` for Google Play Console
Google Play requires a signed **Android App Bundle (`.aab`)** rather than a debug `.apk`, with a unique non-`com.example` `applicationId` (`io.github.elizabackrooms.<slug>`) and `targetSdk = 35`.

### Step A: Generate an Upload Keystore (Once per app or shared upload key)
```powershell
keytool -genkey -v -keystore upload-keystore.jks -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000 -alias upload
```
*(Keep `upload-keystore.jks` and its passwords safely backed up outside version control.)*

### Step B: Configure Release Signing in `app/build.gradle.kts`
Inside the downloaded Android Studio project, verify `defaultConfig`:
- `applicationId = "io.github.elizabackrooms.<app_slug>"`
- `targetSdk = 35`
- `versionCode = 1`
- `versionName = "1.0.0"`

Add a `signingConfigs` block (reading from `keystore.properties` or environment variables) and assign it to `buildTypes.release`.

### Step C: Build the Signed Release `.aab`
Run from the extracted project root:
```powershell
.\gradlew.bat bundleRelease
```
The signed Play Store bundle is output at:
`app\build\outputs\bundle\release\app-release.aab`

### Step D: Upload to Google Play Console
1. Create the app in **Google Play Console** using the exact metadata from `days/day_NN_<slug>/play_store_listing.md`.
2. Fill out the **Data safety**, **App content**, and **Permissions declaration** sections using the pre-filled answers in `play_store_listing.md`.
3. Upload `app-release.aab` to **Internal testing** or **Production**, review pre-launch reports, and submit.
